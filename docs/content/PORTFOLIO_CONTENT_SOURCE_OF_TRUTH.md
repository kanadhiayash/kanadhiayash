## 5. Voice and writing rules

The portfolio voice is direct, calm, specific, evidence-led, technically literate, and honest about limits.

Writing rules:

- Lead with the user, problem, or decision.
- Prefer active voice and short paragraphs.
- Explain why a decision was made.
- Use technical terms only when they improve precision.
- State project status and ownership clearly.
- Separate verified behavior from planned work.
- Keep public labels understandable without learning Eclipse Atlas vocabulary.

Avoid hype, title inflation, vague tool lists, invented user quotes, unsupported metrics, and claims that imply production use.

Terms such as `live`, `shipped`, `production-ready`, `secure`, `scalable`, `validated`, and `used by` require direct evidence.

## 6. Evidence and claims policy

### Claim classes

- **Verified fact:** a direct source supports the exact wording.
- **Supported inference:** evidence supports a reasonable interpretation, but not a measured result.
- **Assumption:** plausible but untested. Keep it out of public claims.
- **Unknown:** no reliable source exists or sources conflict.
- **Risk:** wording could mislead, expose private data, overstate ownership, or imply production use.

### Metric gate

A public metric requires a source, source location, baseline, final value, time period, calculation, sample size, contribution boundary, known confounders, reproducibility check, interview-defensibility check, and public-safety decision.

When methodology is unclear:

```text
Remove the number.
Describe the artifact, decision, responsibility, or observed behavior instead.
```

Never:

- Convert an observation into a percentage.
- Present synthetic tests as real-user results.
- Treat AI review as independent validation.
- Invent participant quotations.
- Present a prototype as a deployed product.
- Present collaborator work as personal ownership.

## 19. Content workflow and quality gate

Workflow:

1. Identify the page and audience.
2. Inventory evidence.
3. Separate facts, supported inferences, assumptions, unknowns, and risks.
4. Draft the smallest complete narrative.
5. Add ownership and status.
6. Link important claims to evidence.
7. Remove unsupported metrics.
8. Review privacy and attribution.
9. Review accessibility and reading order.
10. Verify links, names, dates, and status.
11. Open a reviewable pull request.
12. Merge only after explicit approval.

A page is ready only when Product Designer leads, AI remains a specialization, code remains implementation fluency, ownership and status are explicit, limitations are present, every metric has a source, no invented quote appears, and no private or unresolved information is exposed.
